# Norwegian lineage-register conventions

These conventions were read from the introductory pages of the Follese “Bakkenslekta” register and are useful when the same register format is encountered again.

- A four-digit person number encodes the register generation (`slektsledd`) in the first two digits and sibling order in the final two digits.
- A complete `slektsnummer` is a concatenation of ancestor numbers, family numbers, and the person's own number.
- `ANER:` introduces the active ancestor-number prefix. The prefix remains in effect until a new one is printed.
- A family number is normally shown before the eldest child in a sibling group. Later children inherit that family context and receive subsequent person numbers.
- Numbered individuals are people born into the documented family branches. Spouses/partners and some non-lineal children may be unnumbered or marked `adp.`, `fob.`, `ekb.`, or similar.
- `a)`, `b)`, etc. distinguish multiple spouses/partners and can also appear in children's lineage references.
- Dates normally use `dd.mm.yyyy`; a year alone is partial data. `0` or `+` after a death entry indicates known death without an exact date.
- Place columns can describe birth context, residence, or previous residences. Slashes separate multiple place contexts and should not automatically be interpreted as separate events.
- Source codes are defined in the register's source legend. Preserve them as notes or citations unless their exact meaning is unambiguous.
- The register itself warns that it may contain errors, omissions, approximate dates, and information transmitted through third parties.

When OCR turns a qualified lineage number such as `4210c` into text, use the numeric portion for stable identity and retain the qualifier in the raw source note.
